# Preparation and Debrief

## Preparation Intake

Gather only what is material. Unknowns can remain explicit.

| Area | Questions |
|------|-----------|
| Decision | What must be decided, by whom, and by when? |
| Issues | Which terms are negotiable besides price? |
| Interests | What outcomes matter most, and why? |
| Alternatives | What happens if there is no agreement? How can that alternative improve? |
| Authority | Who can approve, sign, or change each term? |
| Counterpart | What pressures, interests, and alternatives might they have? Which are facts versus hypotheses? |
| Criteria | What market data, precedent, policy, expert view, or principle is credible to both sides? |
| Relationship | Is this a one-time transaction or an ongoing relationship? |
| Process | What is the agenda, channel, sequence, and deadline? |

## Set the Range

- **Aspiration:** A strong, supportable outcome worth pursuing.
- **Opening:** The first proposal or request, supported by criteria and chosen with anchoring risk in mind.
- **Reservation point:** The least favorable acceptable package before choosing the BATNA.
- **BATNA:** The course of action if no agreement is reached.

Do not derive the reservation point from aspiration alone. Evaluate the complete package, switching costs, probabilities, time, risk, and relationship impact. Keep uncertain estimates as ranges.

## Build Packages

List issues and rank them for both sides:

| Issue | User priority | Estimated counterpart priority | Possible trade |
|-------|---------------|--------------------------------|----------------|
| Compensation | High | Medium | Base, bonus, equity, review date |
| Timing | Medium | High | Earlier start for another term |
| Risk | Medium | High | Milestone or contingency |

Create two or three packages that are roughly equal in value to the user but differ in structure. Presenting multiple equivalent offers can reveal preferences without giving away value.

## Plan the Conversation

Prepare:

1. A neutral opening that names the shared purpose.
2. Three questions that test the most important assumptions.
3. A concise explanation of the user's interests and criteria.
4. Two or three packages.
5. Conditional concession rules.
6. Responses to the two most likely objections.
7. A pause line and an exit line.
8. A method for confirming authority and documenting agreement.

## Rehearsal Plan

Run four short rounds:

1. Opening and information gathering
2. Extreme anchor or rejection
3. Conditional trade across multiple issues
4. Summary and commitment

After each round, assess:

- Did the user learn something before conceding?
- Did the response address interests rather than only positions?
- Was movement conditional and reciprocal?
- Did the language sound natural for this user and relationship?
- Did the user preserve the ability to pause or walk away?

## Closing Checklist

Confirm:

- Exact terms and units
- Scope and exclusions
- Dates, milestones, and dependencies
- Owners and decision authority
- Payment or performance contingencies
- What is tentative versus final
- How changes and disputes will be handled
- When and how the agreement will be documented

## Debrief

Reconstruct the sequence without hindsight claims:

1. What was offered, asked, conceded, and agreed?
2. What new information changed the strategy?
3. Which interests were met or left unresolved?
4. Is the agreement better than the BATNA and above the reservation point?
5. What language or move helped, and what created risk?
6. What must be confirmed in writing?
7. What should change before the next round?

End with a written recap that separates final terms, tentative terms, open questions, owners, and deadlines.
