# Researching Objective Criteria

Use research when current external facts would materially change the strategy. Do not browse merely to decorate advice.

## Research Standard

1. Define the decision the evidence must support.
2. Search current, relevant, and comparable sources.
3. Prefer primary and authoritative sources where available.
4. Record geography, date, scope, sample, and definition.
5. Triangulate important numbers rather than relying on one aggregator.
6. Cite the source next to the claim.
7. Report ranges and uncertainty; do not create false precision.
8. Separate verified facts from hypotheses about the counterpart.

Do not seek private, sensitive, or invasive information to manufacture leverage. Do not infer motives such as financial distress, illness, divorce, or immigration status without a lawful, relevant, user-provided basis.

## Compensation

Research:

- Employer-posted compensation range for the exact role and location
- Government labor or wage data
- Reputable industry compensation surveys
- Comparable level, scope, geography, and total compensation components
- Company filings or public compensation philosophy where relevant

Useful searches:

```text
site:company-domain.example "role title" "salary range"
[role] compensation survey [location] [current year]
[government labor source] [occupation] wage [location]
```

Distinguish base, bonus, commission, equity, benefits, and one-time payments. Do not mix levels or locations without adjustment.

## Vendor or Commercial Contract

Research:

- Published pricing and packaging
- Comparable suppliers and switching requirements
- Public procurement awards or rate cards where relevant
- Standard service levels, renewal terms, and price-adjustment clauses
- Vendor financial filings and product notices when material

Compare total cost, implementation, risk, lock-in, support, and exit cost, not only list price.

## Real Estate

Research:

- Recent comparable transactions from reliable listing or public-record sources
- Property condition, size, location, and sale date
- Days on market and price changes
- Taxes, assessments, inspection issues, financing, and closing terms
- Local rules that materially affect the transaction

Avoid treating listing prices as completed-sale evidence. Encourage qualified legal, inspection, tax, or brokerage review where needed.

## Dispute or Settlement

Research:

- The governing contract and documented events
- Applicable process, deadlines, and authority
- Public statutes, regulations, or court rules
- Comparable outcomes only when truly comparable and lawfully available
- Expected time, cost, probability, and relationship impact of alternatives

Legal consequences are jurisdiction-specific. Present research as decision support, not legal advice, and recommend counsel for material decisions.
