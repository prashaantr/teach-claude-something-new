# Live Coaching Protocol

Use this protocol when the user is negotiating now or expects rapid advice between exchanges.

## Establish the Channel

At the first practical opportunity, clarify the input source:

- Text updates typed by the user
- Pasted chat or email excerpts
- Notes taken during a call
- A transcript supplied by an explicit tool

Do not imply access to anything else. If the user says "listen to this call" and no audio or transcript tool is available, say that you cannot hear the call and ask them to paste or summarize the latest exchange. Do not recommend covert or unlawful recording.

## Fast Intake

Use what the user already supplied. Ask at most one blocking question at a time. The highest-value facts are:

1. Their exact latest words
2. The decision or response expected now
3. The latest offer and prior offer
4. The user's target, reservation point, and authority
5. Any deadline or relationship constraint

If a fact is missing but advice cannot wait, give a holding line first:

> "I want to consider that carefully. Can we take a few minutes and come back to it?"

Then ask for the missing fact.

## Response Contract

Default to:

```text
Say: "[one sentence]"
Then: [one question, silence, pause, or package]
Avoid: [only the most likely immediate error]
```

Keep it short enough to scan during a conversation. Do not front-load theory.

When interpretation is uncertain:

```text
If this is a hard authority limit:
Say: "..."

If this is bargaining pressure:
Say: "..."
```

When a number is requested but the user's floor or alternatives are unknown:

```text
Say: "Before I react to the number, can you walk me through how you arrived at it?"
Need from you: target, walk-away point, and previous offers.
```

## Update the Ledger After Every Exchange

Capture only new information:

- New position or offer
- Stated interest or constraint
- Concession and what was received for it
- Deadline or authority claim
- Tentative commitment
- Question still unanswered

Label statements correctly:

- **Reported fact:** The user says the counterpart stated it.
- **Verified fact:** Supported by a document, message, or reliable source.
- **Hypothesis:** A possible motive or tactic, not established truth.

## Next-Move Logic

Choose the smallest useful move:

1. **Clarify** when terms, authority, or reasoning are unclear.
2. **Acknowledge** when emotion or identity is blocking information exchange.
3. **Test** a claimed constraint with a calibrated question.
4. **Package** multiple issues instead of conceding one item at a time.
5. **Trade** rather than give: make each concession conditional.
6. **Pause** when the user lacks authority, information, or emotional control.
7. **Close** only when commitments are specific and implementable.

## Concession Discipline

- Do not bid against the user by improving their offer before the other side responds.
- Never reveal the reservation point as a default tactic.
- Reduce concession size as the negotiation progresses when appropriate.
- Attach a reason to movement and seek reciprocity.
- Use conditional language: "If you can do X, I can consider Y."
- Record every concession; repeated small concessions can become a large package.

## Common Live Events

### Extreme anchor

```text
Say: "Help me understand the assumptions behind that number."
Then: Ask which objective criteria they used.
Avoid: Countering immediately without a basis.
```

### "Final offer"

```text
Say: "What constraint makes that final, and what could change it?"
Then: Test authority, scope, timing, or package flexibility.
Avoid: Treating either "final" or "bluff" as established fact.
```

### Unexpected demand

```text
Say: "That's new information. I need to understand its impact before responding."
Then: Ask how it connects to the existing package.
```

### User feels pressured

```text
Say: "I don't want to commit before I understand the full terms. I'll respond by [time]."
Then: Pause and compare the proposal with the ledger.
```

### Apparent agreement

```text
Say: "Let me summarize what I think we agreed, including what remains open."
Then: Confirm owners, dates, contingencies, and written follow-up.
```

## When to Slow Down or Exit

Recommend a pause, outside review, or exit when:

- The user is being asked to sign or commit beyond their authority.
- A material term is ambiguous or newly introduced.
- The agreement is worse than a viable BATNA or crosses the reservation point.
- There is coercion, discrimination, retaliation, fraud, safety risk, or a legal issue.
- The user is too activated to make an informed decision.

The coach should help the user preserve choice. A pause is often a valid next move.
